1. Run a monthly AI quiz. A friendly monthly quiz on AI concepts and real-world applications would keep everyone engaged. If the instructor is open to it, a small prize for the top scorers (for example, a month of a Claude subscription for the top one or two) would make it extra motivating.
2. Add an interview prep room. For students preparing for roles like AI engineer, an interview agent could run mock interviews, asking technical and behavioral questions and giving honest feedback afterward.
3. Publish a weekly AI newsletter in the Library. AI moves so fast that it's hard to keep up. The TA already turns links into announcements, so it could gather the most important papers and news each week into a short newsletter pinned on the Library board.
4. Open a student discussion board in the Library. Students could post their doubts and questions, and classmates could reply, so it works like a small class forum and nobody has to stay stuck alone.
5. Send email reminders for unread messages. If someone leaves you a message in your office and you haven't seen it for more than 3 days, the site could send you an email so important messages don't get missed.
6. Let students customize their avatar. Everyone already has a character on campus, but they all feel a bit generic. Letting students pick their look, a bitmoji-style avatar, or even upload a photo would make the space feel more personal and help classmates recognize each other.
7. Create an end-of-course reflection summary. At the end of the semester, each student could get a personal summary that pulls together all the feedback they received on their assignments, highlighting their strengths and the areas to keep working on.
8. Run a "what did we miss?" debate after each class. Instead of just summarizing the lecture, AI participants could argue over the trickiest points, surface the questions nobody asked, and link the topic back to earlier weeks, giving students a short list of gaps to review.
9. Track where students get confused. Over the semester, the TA could keep note of the topics each student keeps asking about or struggling with. The goal is to catch confusion early: students see exactly what they need to work on, the TA can explain those topics differently, and the instructor can see which concepts the whole class finds hard and revisit them in lecture.
